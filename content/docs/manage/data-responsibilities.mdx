A Cloud pipeline uses resources managed by tapstate and databases that you
connect. Identify who manages each resource, who pays for it, and what must be
retained before running a pipeline against application data.

For personal information handling and privacy requests, read the
[Privacy Policy](https://tapstate.com/legal/privacy). For the terms covering
personal data processed through your pipelines, read the
[Data Processing Addendum](https://tapstate.com/legal/data-processing-addendum).

## Identify who manages each resource

| Resource | Management and cost considerations |
|---|---|
| Hosted Cluster and its platform-provided databases | Tapstate provisions these with the subscription and reclaims them when it ends. Review the [plan and subscription terms](/docs/manage/billing). |
| Source databases you connect | You manage database access, CDC prerequisites, log retention, and the provider's charges. |
| Atlas cache and application databases in your own account | You manage the Atlas project, database users, network access, backups, and provider billing. Keep the durable cache for recovery; connecting these databases does not transfer ownership or billing to tapstate. |

A tapstate trial applies to the Cloud subscription. It does not change billing
for a source database or an Atlas project you manage separately. Likewise,
canceling tapstate does not cancel those providers' subscriptions.

Before choosing storage, confirm which resource a displayed storage allowance
covers. Do not apply a platform-managed storage allowance to an Atlas database
in your own account or treat it as a backup allowance.

## Protect access to your data

- Use a dedicated database account with the permissions in its connector guide.
  Keep Atlas cache and output permissions scoped to the databases the Cluster uses.
- Allow the [Cluster outbound IP](/docs/manage/clusters#prepare-network-access)
  through database network controls. Use TLS with certificate verification;
  do not treat an IP allowlist as a replacement for database authentication.
- Enter credentials in the connection form. Exclude passwords, private keys,
  and connection URIs from shared screenshots, support tickets, and AI prompts.
- Manage organization access in [Settings](/docs/manage/organizations), and
  review who can use the Cluster before connecting production databases.
- Arrange and verify backups for databases you control. Pipeline checkpoints
  and a running status do not prove that a backup exists or can be restored.

For pricing, trials, purchases, and refunds, use [Pricing and billing](/docs/manage/billing).

## Prepare the databases you connect

For each source, provide a reachable endpoint and a database account with the
permissions required by its [connector guide](/docs/connectors). For CDC,
configure and retain the source logs needed to follow changes. Plan log
retention around outages and the time it may take to resume processing.

For an Atlas destination, choose the database and collection that tapstate may
write. Configure network access and database permissions for the
[Cluster's outbound address](/docs/manage/clusters#prepare-network-access).
Use a separate destination for evaluation so test writes cannot change an
application's collection.

Keep credentials within the connection setup workflow. Do not place connection
URIs containing passwords in screenshots, support messages, or documentation.

## Distinguish configuration, recovery state, and output

Source and pipeline definitions describe what tapstate should run. Recovery
positions and operator state let it continue processing. Materialized output is
the data written for consumers. These have different purposes; a copy of one
is not automatically a backup of the others.

Three-layer Cloud setup uses platform-provided Configuration and your Atlas
Durable cache and Application data databases. Keep the cache separate from
output and retain it while pipelines depend on its recovery state. An earlier
Cluster's Atlas output connection does not relocate its internal metadata.

Do not use the older [Move Nest state](/docs/guides/move-nest-state) procedure
for a three-layer installation. See [State and storage](/docs/concepts/storage)
for backup boundaries.

## Verify the data you use

Before connecting a consumer, compare known source records with the actual
output. Check keys, transformed values, related rows, and deletes as applicable.
A preview, a passed connection test, or a **Running** status does not establish
that all intended changes reached the destination.

Use an independent test environment for write and recovery checks. The
[Build a pipeline](/docs/guides/build-a-pipeline#5-check-the-result) guide
includes a small parent/child example with explicit update checks; use
[Observe a pipeline](/docs/guides/observe-a-pipeline) to investigate missing or
stale results.

## AI provider data

If you configure **Tapstate AI**, generation sends your prompt, the draft
configuration, and the included discovered schemas to that provider. Table and
column names, types, keys, and expressions can contain sensitive information.
Use a provider approved by your organization for this data. Do not paste
passwords, private keys, connection strings, or customer records into prompts.

Provider settings belong to the current organization and Cluster. A connection
check contacts the provider without saving the settings. The application does
not return the saved API key to the panel. Check your provider's usage limits and charges before sending requests.
See [Build with AI](/docs/guides/build-a-pipeline#build-with-ai) for the workflow.

## Plan for retention and cancellation

Identify what must remain available if the Cluster stops or its subscription
ends. Review backups and exports for databases you control before changing
infrastructure or subscriptions. Do not treat pipeline recovery state as a
substitute for a database backup.

[Canceling a subscription](/docs/manage/billing#cancel-a-subscription) schedules
its end; it does not immediately remove the Cluster. At the end of the current
trial or paid period, the platform terminates the associated Cluster and
reclaims its platform-provided databases. Cleanup deletes those databases,
including any configuration, recovery state, or output stored there.

For a three-layer installation, the platform-managed Configuration database
contains the Cluster identity, keys, and saved definitions. Preserve the
configuration you need before it is reclaimed. Durable cache and Application
data in your own Atlas account are separate from that platform allocation;
manage their retention, backups, and deletion through your database provider.
On an earlier layout, platform allocations may also hold recovery state or
output. Check the actual storage layout before planning cancellation.

Databases in your own accounts require a separate decision. Cancellation does
not cancel your Atlas service or replace its backup and deletion controls.
Check which collections tapstate writes, retain the output you need, and manage
those resources through the database provider. A tapstate cancellation status
alone is not evidence that customer-managed data was deleted or backed up.

Before ending a subscription:

1. Identify the affected Cluster, pipelines, applications, and period end.
2. Preserve the source and pipeline definitions needed to recreate the work.
   Store any required credentials separately in your credential manager.
3. Back up or export required output using the database's own tools, and check
   that you can read the retained copy.
4. Decide which customer-managed databases to keep, and review their billing
   and access separately after the pipeline stops.

For privacy or data-deletion requests, contact
[privacy@tapstate.com](mailto:privacy@tapstate.com). Identify the organization
and the category of data involved; do not include database credentials or
sensitive records in your initial message. Canceling a subscription is not the
same as requesting deletion of an account or billing records.

Contact [support](/docs/manage/billing#get-help) about backup and restore
options before relying on recovery after cancellation. Preserve required data
before the subscription ends.
